Crawl Space Encapsulation Cost

Most homeowners searching crawl space encapsulation cost want a honest ballpark before scheduling inspections. Professional full encapsulation commonly lands between $5,000 and $15,000+ for a typical crawl — but scope, moisture problems, and local labor swing the number more than averages suggest.

Typical cost ranges (2026 U.S.)

Project type Common range What’s usually included
Basic vapor barrier (floor only) $1,500 – $4,000 6–12 mil liner on ground, minimal wall overlap
Full encapsulation (floor + walls + piers) $5,000 – $15,000+ Liner system, seam tape, sealed vents, often a dehumidifier
High-end / problem crawl $12,000 – $20,000+ Drainage, sump, mold remediation, structural access issues
DIY materials (your labor) $500 – $2,500 Liner, tape, optional dehu — see Reddit cost roundup

National “average cost” pages often cite ~$5,500. Forum threads and homeowner posts more often show single bids of $10k–$18k when mold, drainage, or wall liner is bundled — not because encapsulation always costs that much, but because one-line quotes hide optional upsells.

Price per square foot

Contractors sometimes quote $3–$10 per square foot of crawl floor area. That math only works if you know what the per-foot price includes:

  • Floor liner only vs walls and piers wrapped
  • Mil thickness and brand of polyethylene
  • Seam tape, mechanical fasteners, termite inspection gaps (where required)
  • Dehumidifier sizing, electrical, and condensate routing
  • Pre-work: grading, French drain, sump pump, mold treatment

A low $/sq ft number with a high total usually means a large crawl or a lot of add-ons — ask for a line-item scope, not a lump sum.

What raises encapsulation cost

Factor Typical impact
Standing water / poor drainage Drain tile, sump, or exterior grading before liner — often +$2k–$8k
Mold or fungal growth Remediation vendor, clearance testing — variable; don’t bundle blindly
Low clearance or tight access More labor hours; sometimes pier wrapping is manual
Conditioned / sealed crawl requirements Supply air, sealed vents, code-driven upgrades
Commercial-grade dehumidifier $1,200–$2,500+ installed with dedicated circuit
HVAC or ductwork in crawl Coordination with HVAC; may affect liner routing

Sellers sometimes recommend full encapsulation before fixing water entry. That order can trap moisture under liner. Read drawbacks of encapsulation and fix intrusion first when appropriate.

How to compare quotes (apples to apples)

  1. Measure crawl floor L × W; note height and number of piers
  2. Request the same scope from each bidder: floor, walls, piers, vent strategy, dehu model
  3. Ask for RH readings or moisture logs — not just a visual “it’s wet”
  4. Separate optional lines: mold, sump, electrical, permit fees
  5. Compare warranty length and what maintenance voids coverage

When full encapsulation isn’t worth the price

Not every crawl needs a $12k encapsulation package. Often a lighter alternative — vapor barrier plus dehumidifier, drainage only, or targeted air sealing — solves the actual problem for less. If your quotes cluster above national averages with vague scope, get a second opinion before signing.

Crawl space dehumidifier cost (common add-on)

Many people searching dehumidifier crawl space or encapsulation cost are really pricing the sealed-crawl package: liner + closed vents + a commercial-grade dehumidifier. Treat the dehumidifier as its own line item so you can compare bids.

  • Unit + install (typical): about $1,200–$2,500+ when sized for a sealed crawl, with a dedicated outlet or circuit and a condensate drain or pump.
  • What changes price: crawl volume (not just floor sq ft), how wet the space stays after drainage fixes, ducted vs freestanding units, and whether electrical upgrades are needed.
  • Don’t skip the water problem: a dehumidifier on standing water or active leaks just runs forever. Fix intrusion first — then size the unit after encapsulation or vapor control is in place.

If your “encapsulation” quote hides the dehumidifier inside a lump sum, ask for the model, pint/day rating, and whether condensate is gravity-drained or pumped. That single question often explains a $2k–$3k gap between contractors.

FAQ

Is $8,000 a reasonable crawl space encapsulation cost?

For a mid-size crawl (1,000–1,500 sq ft) with floor and wall liner, sealed vents, and a installed dehumidifier, $6k–$10k is common in many markets. Verify line items; $8k with no dehu or no wall liner may be overpriced — $8k with drainage and mold remediation may be fair.

Why did I get one quote for $18,000?

Usually bundled remediation, premium liner, oversized dehu, or a single vendor with no competition. Always collect at least three bids and check what other homeowners reported in your region.

Does encapsulation increase home value?

It can help sell a home with moisture or odor issues, but ROI is market-specific. Treat encapsulation as moisture control — not a guaranteed appraisal boost.

Can I finance crawl space encapsulation?

Many contractors offer payment plans or partner lenders. Compare APR and total cost; don’t let financing pressure replace scope review.

Is a crawl space dehumidifier required with encapsulation?

Not every code path requires one, but sealed crawls without a drying strategy often stay humid. Most full encapsulation packages include a dehumidifier for a reason — budget it explicitly rather than discovering it as a change order.

Why do online “average cost” pages disagree so much?

They mix vapor-barrier-only jobs with full encapsulation, ignore regional labor, and rarely separate mold or drainage. Prefer ranges plus line items — and local written bids — over a single national average.
